How Does Alpha Picks Work (2026 Guide): Independent Guide

By FinPins · October 16, 2026

Twice a month, Seeking Alpha takes a US stock its quant model has rated Strong Buy for at least 75 trading days in a row, adds it to a model portfolio and tells subscribers. When ratings change, members get Hold or Sell alerts. That is Alpha Picks in a nutshell; below are the exact rules from the help center and how members put them to use.

Eligibility rules

  • Strong Buy quant rating for at least 75 consecutive days.
  • The rating reflects value, growth, profitability, EPS revisions and momentum.
  • US common stock only; ADRs excluded.
  • REITs excluded.
  • Three-month average market capitalisation above $500 million.
  • Share price above $10.
  • Not recommended within the past year.

Why 75 days matters

The persistence requirement screens out stocks that pop into Strong Buy briefly and then fade. It favours companies whose fundamentals and estimate trends have stayed strong for months, which is a sensible guard against noise.

Timing of picks

One pick is published on the first trading day of each month and the second on the 15th or the next trading day. Apple's listing says they arrive in your inbox.

What membership includes

  • Two new picks monthly.
  • Hold and Sell alerts when ratings shift, plus regular performance updates.
  • Members-only webinars and the stock selection research.
  • The full current and historical portfolio.

Quant only

Picks come from the quant model plus the eligibility screens; contributor articles and Wall Street ratings are not the selection mechanism. To understand the engine, read Quant Ratings explained.

Three ways members use it

What it will not do

It does not size positions to your situation, manage taxes, account for your other holdings, or cover REITs and foreign ADRs. Income and international investors may need other tools.

Where people go wrong

  • Treating a Strong Buy rating as a guarantee rather than a summary of data.
  • Reading only the articles that agree with you.
  • Overlooking that fees are generally non-refundable after the trial.
  • Choosing a plan for the investor you wish you were, not the one you are.
  • Skipping the checkout check that confirms $269 before paying.
