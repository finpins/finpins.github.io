What the company claims

Asked whether the ratings are backtested, the support center answers yes and points to the performance page at seekingalpha.com/performance/quant, tested against the S&P 500 Total Return Index. The App Store copy says Strong Buy ratings beat the S&P 500, and the PRO help page claims the ratings have significantly outperformed the market.

Why we do not quote a percentage

Independent write-ups describe the chart as an equal-weighted hypothetical basket of Strong Buy names from 2010 onward, part backtest and part simulation. The live page blocked our fetch and figures change with the date, so instead of repeating a number we could not check, we suggest you read the page and its footnotes yourself.

How the score is built

Stocks are compared with sector peers on more than 100 metrics grouped into Value, Growth, Profitability, Momentum and EPS Revisions, producing a Strong Sell to Strong Buy rating plus A+ to F grades. Full explanation: Quant Ratings explained.

Backtest caveats

  • Nobody actually owned the hypothetical basket, so costs, taxes and slippage are absent.
  • Equal weights and frequent rebalancing are hard to replicate.
  • Models built with hindsight can fit the past better than the future.
  • Start and end dates change results a lot.
  • Methodology choices matter; the footnotes are worth reading.

Where the model struggles

Ratings can swing after earnings when momentum and revisions flip. Binary-event stocks, such as biotech awaiting an approval, are poorly described by financial ratios. Unusual businesses without true peers make sector comparisons weaker. And a Strong Buy is not a price target or timing signal.

Using it sensibly

Treat the rating as a shortlist tool and a warning light. When a holding slips to Sell, find out which grade changed and why before acting. Pair the score with one article and the latest call transcript.
