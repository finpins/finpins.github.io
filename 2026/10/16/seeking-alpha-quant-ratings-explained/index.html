The Five Factor Grades

Value measures how cheap a stock is relative to sector peers on valuation metrics. A high grade means it looks inexpensive versus similar companies.

Growth looks at how fast revenue, earnings and related measures are growing relative to peers.

Profitability captures margins, returns and cash generation compared with the sector.

Momentum reflects price performance relative to peers over various periods.

EPS Revisions tracks whether analysts are raising or lowering earnings estimates, a signal that expectations are improving or deteriorating.

Each grade runs from A+ to F. Reading them together tells you the shape of a stock: cheap but shrinking, expensive but growing fast, highly profitable but losing momentum and so on.

Why Sector-Relative Matters

Comparing a bank's valuation with a software company's makes little sense. By grading each stock against its own sector, the Quant Rating avoids penalizing whole industries for traits that are normal in that industry. A utility is judged against utilities, a semiconductor maker against other chip companies.

Rating Caps: Built-In Guardrails

Seeking Alpha says weak factor grades can cap the overall rating. According to its help center, a grade of D+ or worse in Growth, Momentum or EPS Revisions, or D- or worse in Value or Profitability, can limit how high the rating can go. In practice, a stock cannot reach Strong Buy simply because it is cheap if its fundamentals or estimates are falling apart. That guardrail is designed to reduce the risk of value traps.

How to Use Quant Ratings Well

As a first filter. Before reading anything about a stock, check its rating and factor grades. It takes seconds and frames the questions you should ask.

As a second opinion. If a newsletter, friend or social media post recommends a stock, see whether the quant view agrees. Disagreement is not a veto, but it is a reason to dig deeper.

To monitor holdings. Watch for rating downgrades and falling grades on stocks you own, especially in EPS Revisions and Profitability.

Alongside analysis. The rating tells you what the numbers say; contributor articles and earnings call transcripts tell you why. Use both.

Limitations to Keep in Mind

Quant ratings are backward-looking to the extent that they rely on reported data and current estimates. They can miss qualitative factors such as a new product, a management change or a legal issue, and they can lag sudden shifts. They are also relative: a Strong Buy in a weak sector is still in a weak sector. Treat ratings as an input, not an instruction.

Seeking Alpha Quant Ratings Explained With an Example

Imagine a mid-sized retailer showing a Hold rating with grades of B+ Value, C Growth, A- Profitability, D Momentum and C- EPS Revisions. Reading this, you would conclude the business is very profitable and fairly cheap, but growth is middling, the share price has lagged peers and analysts are trimming estimates. The rating is held back by momentum and revisions, not by business quality. An investor who believes the estimate cuts are temporary might see an opportunity; one who trusts the trend might wait for revisions to turn. That is the real power of having Seeking Alpha Quant Ratings explained factor by factor: they tell you what to investigate.

Seeking Alpha Quant Ratings Explained FAQ

What are Seeking Alpha Quant Ratings?

Computer-generated ratings that compare each stock with sector peers on more than 100 metrics, producing a 1.0–5.0 score from Strong Sell to Strong Buy.

What are the factor grades?

A+ to F grades for Value, Growth, Profitability, Momentum and EPS Revisions, each relative to the stock's sector.

What is a rating cap?

Poor grades in certain factors can limit the overall rating, so a stock cannot earn a top rating if key fundamentals are weak.

Do I need Premium for Quant Ratings?

Yes. Quant Ratings and factor grades are Premium features.

Are Quant Ratings enough to pick stocks?

They are a strong starting point, but combine them with analysis, transcripts and your own judgment.
