Seeking Alpha Quant Ratings Explained (2026 Guide): Scores, Grades & Use

By FinPins · October 16, 2026

Seeking Alpha's Quant Rating is a computer-generated score that compares a stock with others in its sector on more than 100 metrics and condenses the result to a number from 1.0 to 5.0, labelled Strong Sell to Strong Buy. Five factor grades underneath explain the score. It is a core reason people pay for Premium and it also feeds Alpha Picks. Here is how it works, per Seeking Alpha's help center.

  • Score 1.0–5.0; factor grades A+ to F.
  • Weak grades can cap the overall rating.

The five grades

  • Value: how cheap versus sector peers.
  • Growth: revenue and earnings growth versus peers.
  • Profitability: margins, returns and cash generation.
  • Momentum: relative price performance.
  • EPS Revisions: whether analysts are raising or cutting estimates.

Read together, they describe a stock's shape: cheap but shrinking, pricey but fast-growing, and so on.

Why compare within sectors

A bank's valuation means little next to a software firm's. Grading against the stock's own sector stops whole industries being penalised for traits normal to them.

Built-in caps

According to the help center, a D+ or worse in Growth, Momentum or EPS Revisions, or D- or worse in Value or Profitability, can limit how high the rating goes. A cheap company with collapsing fundamentals cannot coast to Strong Buy, which helps against value traps.

Using the ratings well

Use them as a first filter before reading, as a second opinion on tips, and to monitor holdings for downgrades, especially in EPS Revisions and Profitability. Pair the number with analysis and transcripts that explain the why.

Limits

Ratings rely on reported data and current estimates, so they can miss qualitative shifts. They are a tool, not a verdict. Alpha Picks draws on them; see our Alpha Picks review.
