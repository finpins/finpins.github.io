How Zacks Rank works

It ranks stocks largely on changes in earnings estimates, with a Rank #1 list and Focus List for members who act on revisions.

How Seeking Alpha's rating works

Value, Growth, Profitability, Momentum and EPS Revisions grades, scored against sector peers on 100+ metrics, from 1.0 to 5.0, with caps for weak grades. Our quant explainer has the detail.

Beyond the rating

Seeking Alpha adds a large library of contributor analysis and transcripts; Zacks centres on its ranking system and lists.

Premium's features, one by one

  • Contributor analysis: Every premium article in the archive opens up, so you can read the bull case and the bear case on the same ticker instead of one paywalled teaser a month.
  • Earnings call transcripts: Transcripts come with audio, which makes it practical to hear how management answers tough questions rather than relying on a summary.
  • Quant Rating: A single 1.0 to 5.0 score, from Strong Sell to Strong Buy, built by comparing a company with its sector peers on more than 100 metrics.
  • Factor grades: A+ to F grades for Value, Growth, Profitability, Momentum and EPS Revisions show what drives the score; weak grades can cap the rating.
  • Dividend scores: Grades for dividend safety, growth, yield and consistency give income investors a fast first check.
  • Financials and comparisons: Ten years of downloadable financials and peer comparison tools let you line companies up side by side.
  • Portfolio alerts: News, rating changes and alerts follow the holdings you add, so changes come to you.

Strengths on each side

Where Zacks wins: a 30-day free trial against Seeking Alpha's 7 days, a lower $249 list price, and a single focused rank for investors who act on estimate revisions.

Where Seeking Alpha wins: five factor grades instead of one main driver, sector-relative scoring across 100+ metrics, caps that block weak companies from top ratings, and a large library of written analysis and transcripts.

Choosing by budget

Zero budget: use the free tier of Zacks Premium where one exists and Seeking Alpha's Basic account, which includes portfolio tracking, real-time alerts and one premium article a month.

One paid tool: pay for the service that matches your main job. If that job is deciding which individual companies to own, Premium at $269 for year one is the natural pick; if it is the specialty Zacks Premium is known for, choose that.

Two tools: combine a free tier of one with a paid plan of the other, and review after twelve months whether both earned their place. Remember that Premium renews at $299 after year one.

Mistakes to avoid
