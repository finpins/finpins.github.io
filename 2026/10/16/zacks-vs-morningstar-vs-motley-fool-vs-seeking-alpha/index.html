Zacks Premium

Zacks is built around the Zacks Rank, which sorts stocks from #1 Strong Buy to #5 Strong Sell with earnings estimate revisions at the core. Zacks Premium ($249 a year, with a 30-day free trial) adds the full Rank #1 list, the Focus List, equity research reports and pre-built screens. Zacks says its #1 ranked stocks have more than doubled the S&P 500 since 1988 on a hypothetical basis; treat that as a vendor claim. Our head-to-head Seeking Alpha vs Zacks goes deeper.

Morningstar Investor

Morningstar Investor ($249 a year or $34.95 a month, with a 7-day free trial for eligible new users) gives you Morningstar's analyst research, ratings for funds and ETFs, fair-value-based stock analysis and portfolio tools that show allocation and exposure. It is the most fund-friendly of the four and the only one with a monthly plan among these core subscriptions. See Seeking Alpha vs Morningstar for a direct comparison.

Motley Fool Stock Advisor

Stock Advisor ($199 a year list, with introductory pricing for new members and a 30-day membership-fee-back guarantee) has run since 2002 and sends two new stock recommendations a month. It encourages building a diversified long-term portfolio of 25 or more stocks. It is the simplest of the four to use because it tells you what to buy. Compare it directly in Seeking Alpha vs Motley Fool.

Seeking Alpha Premium

Seeking Alpha Premium ($299 a year list, $269 for the first year through our link, with a 7-day free trial for new subscribers) unlocks unlimited analysis from its contributor network, earnings call transcripts with audio, dividend scores and ten years of financials. Its Quant Rating compares each stock with sector peers on more than 100 metrics, producing a 1.0 to 5.0 score with factor grades for Value, Growth, Profitability, Momentum and EPS Revisions. For curated picks, Seeking Alpha sells Alpha Picks separately.

Price Compared

At list price the order from cheapest is Motley Fool Stock Advisor ($199), then Zacks Premium and Morningstar Investor (both $249), then Seeking Alpha Premium ($299). First-year discounts change the picture: Seeking Alpha's offer through our link is $269, and the Motley Fool also runs introductory pricing. Over a year, the spread between all four is roughly $100, small compared with the impact a good or bad research tool can have on a portfolio.

Trials and Guarantees Compared

Zacks has the longest free trial at 30 days. Morningstar and Seeking Alpha each give 7 days free. The Motley Fool takes a different approach with a 30-day membership-fee-back guarantee: you pay first, then can ask for your money back within the window. Seeking Alpha's fees are generally non-refundable after the trial, though you can cancel at any time and keep access to the end of the period.

Method Compared: Four Philosophies

Zacks asks: are analysts raising their earnings estimates? Momentum in expectations drives its rank.

Morningstar asks: what is this business or fund really worth over the long run, and how does it fit your portfolio?

Motley Fool asks: which few companies could be long-term winners? Then it tells you.

Seeking Alpha asks: how does this stock score across value, growth, profitability, momentum and revisions versus its peers, and what are bulls and bears saying?

Which question resonates most with you is a good sign of which service you will actually keep using.

Best Choice by Investor Type

  • Hands-off stock investor: Motley Fool Stock Advisor, or Seeking Alpha's Alpha Picks if you prefer quant-driven selection.
  • Fund and ETF investor: Morningstar Investor.
  • Earnings-momentum trader: Zacks Premium.
  • Self-directed stock picker and dividend investor: Seeking Alpha Premium.
  • Undecided: start with the free trials of Seeking Alpha and Zacks, then compare.
