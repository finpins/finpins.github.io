Seeking Alpha Stock Screener (2026 Guide): How It Works, Filters & Is It Worth It

By FinPins · October 16, 2026

Most stock screeners hand you a blank form and expect you to know which P/E, margin and growth thresholds make sense. Seeking Alpha took a different route: its screener filters on ratings it has already computed, such as quant scores, factor grades, dividend grades and the views of its authors and Wall Street analysts. Getting to it

  1. Choose "Stock Screener" in the left navigation.
  2. Press "Create New Screen".
  3. Pick the lens: highest-rated by Seeking Alpha authors, by sell-side analysts, or by the quant model; optionally narrow by country, sector and industry.
  4. Open Advanced Filters to tighten the list.
  5. Save and name it; manage saved lists under "My Screens", where the bin icon deletes one.

Predefined screens are included to get you started.

Filter families

Advanced filters are grouped into ratings (quant, author, Wall Street), quant factor grades (value, growth, profitability, momentum, revisions), dividend grades (safety, growth, yield, consistency), trading data, earnings data and company details.

Why grades make screening faster

Because every grade is already sector-relative, asking for 'Profitability A- or better' means highly profitable compared with industry peers. With a raw-number tool you would have to set different margin thresholds for each sector yourself. Seeking Alpha pitches it as an easy way to surface stocks and REITs with the strongest ratings.

Four screens worth saving

  • Quality without the premium price: quant Buy or better, profitability A- or better, value C or better.
  • Income with a cushion: dividend safety B+ or better and consistency B or better inside one sector.
  • Rising estimates: revisions A or better plus momentum B or better.
  • Everyone agrees: quant Strong Buy and author rating Buy or better.

A monthly routine

Re-run saved screens once a month and look only at new names. For each, check whether the rating is long-standing or just changed, note the weakest grade and read the latest article. Move survivors to a watchlist so alerts follow them. This keeps the screener an idea generator rather than a trading trigger.

Mistakes we see

Piling on filters until nothing passes, screening on yield without safety, and re-running daily, which invites overtrading because grades shift as data updates.

Compared with free screeners

A free tool like Finviz is excellent for numeric filters and heat maps. Seeking Alpha's edge is that each result links straight to the grades, analysis and transcripts you need next. If numbers alone suffice, stay free; if you want rating-based filters plus instant research, Premium fits. Compare in Seeking Alpha vs Finviz and free alternatives.

Caveats

Results inherit the assumptions of Seeking Alpha's rating system and change as data does. A screen result is a research list, not advice, and Seeking Alpha is not a licensed adviser.

Feature-by-feature walkthrough

  • Unlimited reading: The one-article monthly cap of the free account disappears; the full contributor library is available.

  • Sector-relative scoring: The Quant Rating weighs 100+ metrics against same-sector peers and lands on a 1.0–5.0 score.
  • Five grades under the score: Value, Growth, Profitability, Momentum and EPS Revisions each get a letter from A+ to F.
  • Income tools: Dividend safety, growth, yield and consistency scores help screen payers quickly.
  • Data depth: A decade of downloadable financials, peer comparisons, and earnings and dividend forecast visuals.
  • Watchlists that work for you: Portfolio-linked alerts surface rating changes and news on what you own.
