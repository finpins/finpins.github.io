Why calls beat press releases

The release gives numbers; the call shows how management frames them, what guidance it offers and, above all, how it handles tough analyst questions. Shifts in tone and dodged questions often show up here before they show up in results.

Finding a transcript

Go to the company's symbol page and open its earnings area for the latest call; transcripts also flow through news and analysis feeds. Holdings in your portfolio surface new material automatically through alerts.

AI summaries

Earnings Call Insights condenses calls with AI. Use it to triage which calls deserve a full read, then read the Q&A yourself for anything you own.

Listen or read?

Audio captures hesitation and emphasis; text is searchable and skimmable. A good split: skim text for guidance and key numbers, listen to the Q&A for your largest positions.

A quarter-hour routine

  1. Scan prepared remarks for headline figures and guidance changes.
  2. Search for words such as guidance, margin, demand and headwind.
  3. Read every analyst question on the issue that matters most to you.
  4. Compare phrasing with last quarter's call.
  5. Watch whether the EPS Revisions grade moves over the next few days.

Reading the Q&A like an analyst

Note which questions get numbers and which get generalities; an analyst re-asking a question usually means the first answer fell short. Look for new terms appearing for the first time and softer guidance language, and check whether management addresses risks that bearish contributors have raised.

Linking to other Premium tools

After a call, see whether the quant rating or revisions grade shifts as estimates change, read one bullish and one bearish piece written afterwards, and for dividend payers revisit the Safety grade if cash flow came up. Overview: Premium features.

What each Premium tool does

  • The analysis library: Bulls, bears and a comment section on most listed stocks, including smaller companies.
  • Earnings calls: Full transcripts plus audio for each call.
  • The headline score: The Quant Rating, 1.0 to 5.0, compares each stock with its sector across 100+ metrics.
  • What sits underneath: Letter grades for Value, Growth, Profitability, Momentum and EPS Revisions, with caps for weak spots.
  • For dividend investors: Scores covering safety, growth, yield and consistency.
  • For number crunchers: Ten years of financials to download and side-by-side peer comparisons.
  • Monitoring: Alerts tied to your portfolio and author ratings to judge contributors.

Common traps

  • Signing up by typing the URL, which can bypass link pricing.
  • Expecting PRO-level features from Premium, or paying $2,400 for PRO before testing Premium.
  • Measuring a picks fee in dollars rather than as a share of your portfolio.
  • Not adding holdings on day one, which makes the trial feel empty.
  • Subscribing to two overlapping tools when one free tool would do.

Checklist
