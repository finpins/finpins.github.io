What Is Seeking Alpha?

Seeking Alpha is an investing research platform. Thousands of contributors, from individual investors to professionals, publish analysis on stocks, ETFs and dividends. Seeking Alpha adds news, data, earnings call transcripts and its own computer-generated Quant Ratings. You can use it free with a Basic account or upgrade to Premium for full access.

Step 1: Add Your Portfolio or Watchlist

The first thing to do, free or paid, is add the stocks and ETFs you own or are interested in. Seeking Alpha then organizes news, alerts and analysis around them. This one step turns a noisy website into a personalized research feed.

Step 2: Learn to Read the Quant Rating

With Premium, every stock shows a Quant Rating from Strong Sell to Strong Buy with a score from 1.0 to 5.0, based on more than 100 metrics compared with sector peers. Below it, five factor grades from A+ to F cover Value, Growth, Profitability, Momentum and EPS Revisions.

For beginners, the grades are the easiest way in. A stock with an A in Profitability and a D in Value is a strong business that looks expensive. A stock with an A in Value and an F in EPS Revisions looks cheap, but analysts are cutting estimates, which may explain why. Our Quant Ratings explainer goes deeper.

Step 3: Read Both Sides

Seeking Alpha's superpower is variety of opinion. For any stock you own, read one bullish and one bearish article. You do not need to agree with either; the goal is to understand the main arguments. Check the date, because older articles may be out of date.

Step 4: Use Dividend Scores if You Want Income

If you buy dividend stocks, Premium's dividend scores for safety, growth, yield and consistency are a quick way to spot dividends that may be at risk. A very high yield with a weak safety grade deserves extra caution.

A Simple First-Week Routine

  • Day 1: Add your holdings and watchlist.
  • Day 2: Note the Quant Rating and weakest factor grade for each holding.
  • Day 3: Read one bull and one bear article on your largest position.
  • Day 4: Skim the latest earnings call transcript for one company you own.
  • Day 5: Check dividend scores on any income stocks.
  • Day 6: Use a screener to find one new idea with strong grades.
  • Day 7: Decide whether anything you learned would change a decision.

Mistakes Beginners Make

Treating a rating as an order. Quant Ratings are a starting point, not a buy button.

Reading only articles that agree with you. The bear case is often the most useful thing you read.

Ignoring the date. Analysis from a year ago may not reflect the company today.

Over-trading. More information can tempt you to act more often. Long-term investors usually benefit from patience.
