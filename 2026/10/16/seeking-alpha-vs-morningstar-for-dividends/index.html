Seeking Alpha's income toolkit

Four sector-relative grades sit on every payer's symbol page. Seeking Alpha says Safety draws on 13 metrics including forward cash flow and earnings, Growth blends past raises with projected revenue, profit and free-cash-flow growth, Yield ranks current and projected yield against the sector, and Consistency summarises the pay-and-raise record. Grades refresh daily before the open. The dividend tab adds payout ratio, yield on cost and long history charts. More in our dividend grades guide.

A community built for income

Many contributors specialise in REITs, BDCs, preferreds and dividend growth, and several income-focused Investing Groups, such as High Yield Landlord and The Dividend Kings, appear among Seeking Alpha's in-app purchases. See Investing Groups.

Morningstar's angle

Morningstar built its reputation on valuation and fund analysis. Fair value estimates and moat ratings help you decide whether a payer is a durable business priced sensibly, and its fund research is a go-to for income funds. It does not present dividends as a four-grade card.

Strategy by strategy

  • Dividend growth: Seeking Alpha's Growth and Consistency grades are purpose-built.
  • High yield and REITs: Safety grades plus deep niche commentary favour Seeking Alpha.
  • Fund-based income: Morningstar.
  • Deep value: Morningstar's fair value framework.

Using both

Some investors screen on Seeking Alpha's dividend grades, then sanity-check valuation and moat on Morningstar, and use Morningstar for their fund sleeve. That costs roughly $518 to $548 a year combined, sensible only for a sizeable income portfolio.
