Seeking Alpha App Review (2026 Guide): 4.8 Stars, Free vs Premium In-App

By FinPins · October 16, 2026

We looked at the Seeking Alpha: News & Analysis listing on Apple's App Store in October 2026 and at Seeking Alpha's own support pages to review the mobile app. It scores highly with users, costs nothing to install and works well as a portfolio tracker. The research depth that people actually pay for sits behind Premium, though, and buying that subscription inside the app is not the cheapest way to get it.

Quick verdict

  • 4.8 stars from roughly 126K iOS ratings.
  • Free download; Premium is an optional in-app purchase.
  • In-app Premium: $49.00 monthly or $299.00 annual per Apple's list.
  • Web partner offer is lower in year one: $269 via the Premium offer link.

Key facts from Apple's listing

  • Publisher: Seeking Alpha Ltd.
  • Rating: 4.8/5, about 126,000 ratings (US store).
  • Price: free with in-app purchases.
  • In-app items include Premium Monthly $49.00, Premium Annual $299.00 and Alpha Picks $499.00.
  • Needs iOS or iPadOS 15.6 or newer; runs on iPhone and iPad.
  • English plus eight other languages.

An Android build is on Google Play too, and Seeking Alpha's help center explains downloading on both platforms.

What you get without paying

Apple's description of Basic lists one Premium article a month, real-time prices, a self-built portfolio, real-time alerts on followed tickers, Wall Street ratings on every stock and limited access to other material. As a free watchlist with news, that is solid.

What a subscription adds on your phone

Premium in the app brings unlimited contributor content, Quant Ratings, ranked lists of stocks and ETFs, stock and ETF screeners, a portfolio health check with warnings, audio playback of articles and webinars. Factor grades, dividend scorecards and call transcripts appear on symbol pages as well. The complete list is in our Premium features rundown.

The pricing trap to avoid

Twelve months of the $49 in-app monthly plan totals $588, about twice the annual list price. The annual in-app option matches list at $299, while the web partner route via the Premium offer link charges $269 for the first year and starts with a free week. Subscribe on the website and simply sign into the app with the same login; access carries over. Full maths: annual versus monthly billing.

Strengths

  • Built around your holdings: news, alerts and rating changes surface automatically.
  • Live prices and alerts even for free users.
  • Ratings and grades are readable on a small screen.
  • Listening to articles suits commutes.
  • Consistently high user score.

Weaknesses users mention

Scanning the written reviews, a long-standing subscriber disliked a redesign rolled out early in 2025 and later described slowdowns and freezes. Another found the notifications excessive and the paywall frustrating. Seeking Alpha replies by pointing people to appsupport@seekingalpha.com. These are anecdotes rather than data, but worth knowing: tune your alerts, and expect the free tier to nudge you toward upgrading.

Privacy label

Apple's privacy section says usage data may be used to track you across other companies' apps and sites, and that purchase history, contact details, user content, searches, identifiers, usage and diagnostics may be tied to you. That is typical of free finance apps.

Getting set up well

Create a portfolio with your real positions first, then switch off every notification type except rating changes and news on holdings. Use the listen feature for long reads. If you are trialling Premium, do it on the web via the Premium offer link so your first year is cheaper, then use the app daily.

Who will like it

Investors who want alerts and quick checks on the go will enjoy the app. Heavy screeners and spreadsheet users will still prefer the desktop site, though one account covers both. If you want to understand whether the company behind it is trustworthy, see is Seeking Alpha legit.

Feature-by-feature walkthrough

  • Unlimited reading: The one-article monthly cap of the free account disappears; the full contributor library is available.
  • Transcripts with audio: Listen to or read earnings calls in full, useful during reporting season.
  • Sector-relative scoring: The Quant Rating weighs 100+ metrics against same-sector peers and lands on a 1.0–5.0 score.
  • Five grades under the score: Value, Growth, Profitability, Momentum and EPS Revisions each get a letter from A+ to F.
  • Income tools: Dividend safety, growth, yield and consistency scores help screen payers quickly.
  • Data depth: A decade of downloadable financials, peer comparisons, and earnings and dividend forecast visuals.
  • Watchlists that work for you: Portfolio-linked alerts surface rating changes and news on what you own.

Where people go wrong

  • Signing up by typing the URL, which can bypass link pricing.
  • Expecting PRO-level features from Premium, or paying $2,400 for PRO before testing Premium.
  • Measuring a picks fee in dollars rather than as a share of your portfolio.
  • Not adding holdings on day one, which makes the trial feel empty.
  • Subscribing to two overlapping tools when one free tool would do.

Five questions before you pay

  • Which job am I hiring this for: research, screening, news or picks?
  • Is the free tier of another service or Seeking Alpha already enough?
  • Can I test with a trial or guarantee before committing?
  • What will year two cost?
  • Would I notice if I cancelled?
