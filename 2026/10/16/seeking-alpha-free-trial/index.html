Seeking Alpha Free Trial (2026 Guide): 7 Days Free, Then $269 First Year

By FinPins · October 16, 2026

Seeking Alpha gives new Premium subscribers seven days free, and if you keep the plan after starting from our link, year one costs $269 rather than the $299 list price. The catch is that a week disappears fast, and Seeking Alpha allows only one free trial per service per lifetime, so it pays to go in with a plan. This page gives you one.

Quick verdict

  • 7 days of full Premium at no cost for new subscribers.
  • Keep it and the first year is $269 via the Premium offer link (list $299).
  • Cancel from account settings before day seven to avoid a charge.
  • One free trial per service per lifetime, so time it well.

What happens when you sign up

The trial sits on the Premium offer. Open the Premium offer link, log in or create an account and add a payment method. You then have seven days of Premium. Do nothing and the plan continues at $269 for the first year; cancel inside the week and you are not billed.

A seven-day test plan

  • Day 1: load your holdings into a portfolio so ratings and alerts appear.
  • Days 2–3: read the analysis and Quant Ratings on what you own.
  • Day 4: run a screen on an idea you have been sitting on.
  • Day 5: compare two stocks side by side.
  • Day 6: keep or cancel.

If you only logged in once, that is your answer.

Still deciding?

The trial costs nothing; cancel before day seven if it is not for you.

Stopping before the charge

Cancel from account settings, ideally on day six rather than the last day so time zones cannot trip you up. Our cancellation guide covers the full rules, including what happens after a paid period starts.

Trials on other products

The week-long trial belongs to Premium. Alpha Picks is a separate service at $499/yr list, shown at $449 via the Alpha Picks offer link as of Oct 2026; the Premium + Alpha Picks bundle link combines both. If you are unsure which you need, read Premium vs Alpha Picks first.

Who should skip it

If you cannot set aside time during the next week, postpone the trial; you only get one. Index-only investors should probably not bother at all.
