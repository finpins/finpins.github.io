Morningstar's strengths

Analyst research, fund ratings and portfolio tools make it the natural home for investors who mostly own funds and want to check fees, holdings and overlap.

Seeking Alpha's strengths

Quant Ratings, factor grades, a huge pool of contributor analysis and dividend tools, aimed at deciding whether to own a specific company. See our Premium review.

Monthly vs annual

Morningstar lets you pay monthly if you would rather not commit; current Seeking Alpha Premium offers are annual.

What each Premium tool does

  • The analysis library: Bulls, bears and a comment section on most listed stocks, including smaller companies.
  • Earnings calls: Full transcripts plus audio for each call.
  • The headline score: The Quant Rating, 1.0 to 5.0, compares each stock with its sector across 100+ metrics.
  • What sits underneath: Letter grades for Value, Growth, Profitability, Momentum and EPS Revisions, with caps for weak spots.
  • For dividend investors: Scores covering safety, growth, yield and consistency.
  • For number crunchers: Ten years of financials to download and side-by-side peer comparisons.
  • Monitoring: Alerts tied to your portfolio and author ratings to judge contributors.

Head to head: strengths

Where Morningstar wins: fund and ETF research, portfolio analysis across funds, and a $34.95 monthly option for people who will not commit to a year. Its $249 list price is also $50 below Premium's list.

Where Seeking Alpha wins: individual-stock depth — Quant Ratings against sector peers, factor grades with rating caps, dividend scores, transcripts with audio and a large pool of opposing views on the same company.

If money is tight

Zero budget: use the free tier of Morningstar Investor where one exists and Seeking Alpha's Basic account, which includes portfolio tracking, real-time alerts and one premium article a month.

One paid tool: pay for the service that matches your main job. If that job is deciding which individual companies to own, Premium at $269 for year one is the natural pick; if it is the specialty Morningstar Investor is known for, choose that.

Two tools: combine a free tier of one with a paid plan of the other, and review after twelve months whether both earned their place. Remember that Premium renews at $299 after year one.

Common traps

  • Signing up by typing the URL, which can bypass link pricing.
  • Expecting PRO-level features from Premium, or paying $2,400 for PRO before testing Premium.
  • Measuring a picks fee in dollars rather than as a share of your portfolio.
  • Not adding holdings on day one, which makes the trial feel empty.
  • Subscribing to two overlapping tools when one free tool would do.

Checklist

  • Is my portfolio big enough that the fee is a small percentage?
  • Do I understand what is and is not included?
  • Have I checked the checkout total before entering card details?
  • Do I know how to cancel and when renewal happens?
  • Have I ignored any unverified holiday discount claims?
