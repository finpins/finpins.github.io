Seeking Alpha Refund Policy (2026 Guide): Trials, Cancellations & the 30-Day Rule

By FinPins · October 16, 2026

Short answer first: Seeking Alpha generally does not hand money back once a subscription period has started. Its knowledge base and Article 20 of the Terms of Use both state that fees are non-refundable unless something different is promised in writing. Cancelling switches off the next renewal; it does not reverse the charge you already paid. Knowing that, the timing of your trial and your cancellation matters far more than any refund request.

Quick verdict

  • Paid periods: no refunds unless agreed in writing.
  • Cancel any time; it bites at the end of the current period.
  • Free trial: cancel one day before it ends or you are billed.
  • Only written refund exception: 30-day guarantee on first-time annual Investing Group plans.

Every rule in one place

  • After payment: non-refundable, for both monthly and annual plans.
  • Cancellation: allowed whenever you like, effective when the current billing period ends.
  • Renewal: automatic for every subscription unless you cancel.
  • Free trials: one per service per lifetime; a repeat sign-up is charged straight away.
  • Paid trials: the trial fee is not refundable and the plan rolls to the normal rate.
  • Investing Groups, annual, first time: cancel within 30 days and get the remaining 11 months back.
  • Investing Groups, monthly: not refundable.
  • Cheaper services such as newsletters or Investing Group Basic: charged up front, no trial, no guarantee.
  • Accounts cancelled for breaching the terms: no refund.

A realistic example

Say you pay for a year of Premium in March and by May you are barely logging in. You can cancel in May, and you will still have Premium until next March, but the policy gives you no partial money back. The Terms of Use put it bluntly: once the initial paid subscription starts, Seeking Alpha does not refund initial orders or recurring payments. Alpha Picks works the same way, as covered in our Alpha Picks cancellation guide.

The trial is the real protection

Because charged periods stay charged, the seven-day Premium trial is where you protect yourself. Look up your trial end date under Account Settings, which lists trial end, next bill date and renewal amount. Cancel at least one day before it expires, as the terms require. Join through an existing member's referral link and the free period becomes fourteen days.

Paid trials are purchases

PRO and some other products have used a cheap paid trial in place of a free one. Seeking Alpha's help article is clear that this fee is not returned and that the plan bills at its normal rate unless stopped before the trial finishes. Budget for it as money spent.

The Investing Group exception

Analyst-run Investing Groups carry the only written guarantee we found. If you are new to a particular group and choose annual billing, cancelling inside the first 30 days refunds the 11 unused months, provided you cancel no later than a day before the 30-day window closes. Monthly group plans and lower-priced services are excluded. Premium, Alpha Picks and PRO are not covered on the pages we read. More in our Investing Groups explainer.

Cancelling without surprises

Sign in, open Account Settings from your avatar and cancel from the subscriptions panel; the terms also accept an email to subscriptions@seekingalpha.com. Keep the confirmation. Full steps for web and app are in how to cancel Seeking Alpha.

Charged in error?

The policy does not promise anything here, but support exists: subscriptions@seekingalpha.com and the phone number printed on Seeking Alpha's price notices, 1-347-509-6837. If you subscribed on the wrong account or did not intend to renew, write quickly and attach the invoice. Whether money comes back is Seeking Alpha's call.

Bought through Apple?

An iPhone in-app subscription is billed by Apple, so it is cancelled in your Apple ID subscription settings and any refund request goes through Apple's own process.

Premium's features, one by one

  • Contributor analysis: Every premium article in the archive opens up, so you can read the bull case and the bear case on the same ticker instead of one paywalled teaser a month.
  • Earnings call transcripts: Transcripts come with audio, which makes it practical to hear how management answers tough questions rather than relying on a summary.
  • Quant Rating: A single 1.0 to 5.0 score, from Strong Sell to Strong Buy, built by comparing a company with its sector peers on more than 100 metrics.
  • Factor grades: A+ to F grades for Value, Growth, Profitability, Momentum and EPS Revisions show what drives the score; weak grades can cap the rating.
  • Dividend scores: Grades for dividend safety, growth, yield and consistency give income investors a fast first check.
  • Financials and comparisons: Ten years of downloadable financials and peer comparison tools let you line companies up side by side.
  • Portfolio alerts: News, rating changes and alerts follow the holdings you add, so changes come to you.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Treating a Strong Buy rating as a guarantee rather than a summary of data.
  • Reading only the articles that agree with you.
  • Overlooking that fees are generally non-refundable after the trial.
  • Choosing a plan for the investor you wish you were, not the one you are.
  • Skipping the checkout check that confirms $269 before paying.
