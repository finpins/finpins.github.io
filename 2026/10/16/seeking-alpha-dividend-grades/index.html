The scorecard, grade by grade

  • Safety estimates the risk of a forced dividend cut. Seeking Alpha says it weighs 13 financial metrics, forward cash-flow and earnings estimates among them, against other companies.
  • Growth looks ahead: past raises plus projected revenue, profit and free-cash-flow growth.
  • Yield ranks today's yield and analysts' projected yield one and two years out against sector peers.
  • Consistency rolls the history of paying and raising the dividend into one grade.

Why sector comparison helps

A yield that looks generous for a software company is unremarkable for a pipeline or a utility. Grading inside the sector stops a portfolio drifting entirely into the highest-yielding industries and helps you find the best income name within each one.

How these differ from the Quant Rating

Seeking Alpha explains that dividend grades are designed around income and income growth, not share-price direction, so they leave out momentum and estimate revisions, which feed the Quant Rating. They also only exist for companies that pay dividends. That is why a stock can score an A for safety and still carry a Hold or Sell quant rating. Who is behind them

Per the support article, the quant ratings, factor grades and dividend grades are designed and run by Steven Cress, Seeking Alpha's VP of Quantitative Strategy and Market Data, who has over three decades in equity research and founded Cress Capital Management.

More on the dividend tab

Below the grades you will see yield on cost, yield history, growth history, payout ratio and long payout charts. Forecasts come from FactSet consensus and run two to three years ahead.

Reading a profile: an example

Picture a consumer-staples name with Safety A-, Growth B, Yield C+ and Consistency A. Translation: the payout looks well covered, raises are likely to continue at a decent pace, the yield is middling for its sector and the record of paying and raising is long. A dividend-growth investor might like it; someone needing maximum current income might pass.

Habits that work

  • Check Safety before getting excited about a high yield.
  • Weight Growth or Yield according to whether you are building income or living on it.
  • Treat Consistency as background, never a guarantee.
  • Glance at the Quant Rating to see how the market currently views the stock.
  • Read a recent article before you buy.
