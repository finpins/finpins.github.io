What Finviz Does Best

Finviz is built for speed. Its screener lets you filter thousands of US stocks by fundamentals, technicals and descriptive data in seconds, and its market map shows the day's winners and losers visually by sector. Much of this is free. Finviz Elite adds real-time quotes, advanced and intraday charts, multi-layout charts, intraday maps and more screener capability. Details are on Finviz Elite.

What Seeking Alpha Does Best

Seeking Alpha goes deep on each company. Premium unlocks unlimited analysis from its contributor network, earnings call transcripts with audio, dividend scores, peer comparisons and ten years of downloadable financials. The Quant Rating compares each stock with sector peers on more than 100 metrics and produces a 1.0 to 5.0 score, with factor grades from A+ to F for Value, Growth, Profitability, Momentum and EPS Revisions. It is designed to help you understand and judge a business, not just find it.

Price: Seeking Alpha vs Finviz

This is a rare near-tie. Finviz Elite costs $299.50 a year, or $39.50 billed monthly. Seeking Alpha Premium lists at $299 a year, and the first year through our link is $269. Finviz offers monthly flexibility; Seeking Alpha is cheaper in year one.

Both have useful free tiers. Finviz's free screener is one of the best free tools in investing, and Seeking Alpha's Basic account includes portfolio tracking, alerts and one premium article a month. Seeking Alpha's paid fees are generally non-refundable once charged, though you can cancel any time and keep access to the end of the period.

Finding vs Judging

The real difference in Seeking Alpha vs Finviz is the stage of research each serves. Finviz is superb at finding: show me profitable mid-caps with low debt, rising earnings and a yield above a threshold, and it delivers a list instantly.

Seeking Alpha is superb at judging: for each name on that list, it tells you how it rates against peers, whether estimates are being revised up or down, what the dividend safety looks like and what thoughtful analysts are arguing. A screen can tell you a stock looks cheap; analysis tells you why it might be cheap.

The Popular Combination

Because Finviz's free screener is so capable, a very common setup is free Finviz plus paid Seeking Alpha Premium. You screen on Finviz, then take the shortlist to Seeking Alpha for quant ratings, factor grades and analysis. That gives you most of the benefits of both for the price of one subscription.

Finviz Elite is worth paying for mainly if you trade actively and need real-time data and intraday charts.

Common Mistakes When Choosing

Many investors pay for Finviz Elite when the free screener already covers their needs, or skip research entirely because a screen looked convincing. A screen is only as good as the questions behind it: a stock can pass every numeric filter and still face a problem that only shows up in an earnings call or analyst debate. Before paying for either, run a free Finviz screen, take the top five results and try researching them on Seeking Alpha's free trial. You will quickly see which half of the process you are missing.

Mobile and Daily Use

Finviz is primarily a desktop web tool, and its dense tables shine on a large screen. Seeking Alpha's app is built around your portfolio, pushing alerts, rating changes and analysis on holdings to your phone. If you research mostly at a desk, either works; if you want to stay on top of holdings on the go, Seeking Alpha fits that habit better.

Seeking Alpha vs Finviz: Our Verdict

For most long-term investors, the best value is Finviz's free screener plus Seeking Alpha Premium. Seeking Alpha gives you the analysis and quant ratings that drive good decisions, and Finviz's free tools cover screening. Choose Finviz Elite instead only if you trade actively and need real-time data or advanced intraday charts.
