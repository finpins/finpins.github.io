What each Premium tool does

  • The analysis library: Bulls, bears and a comment section on most listed stocks, including smaller companies.
  • Earnings calls: Full transcripts plus audio for each call.
  • The headline score: The Quant Rating, 1.0 to 5.0, compares each stock with its sector across 100+ metrics.
  • What sits underneath: Letter grades for Value, Growth, Profitability, Momentum and EPS Revisions, with caps for weak spots.
  • For dividend investors: Scores covering safety, growth, yield and consistency.
  • For number crunchers: Ten years of financials to download and side-by-side peer comparisons.
  • Monitoring: Alerts tied to your portfolio and author ratings to judge contributors.

Head to head: strengths

Where TIKR wins: global financial data and estimates for building your own models, a free tier, and a 14-day money-back guarantee.

Where Seeking Alpha wins: ready-made ratings, factor grades and debate, especially on US stocks and dividend payers, which save time when you do not want to build every model yourself.

If money is tight

Zero budget: use the free tier of TIKR Plus where one exists and Seeking Alpha's Basic account, which includes portfolio tracking, real-time alerts and one premium article a month.

One paid tool: pay for the service that matches your main job. If that job is deciding which individual companies to own, Premium at $269 for year one is the natural pick; if it is the specialty TIKR Plus is known for, choose that.

Two tools: combine a free tier of one with a paid plan of the other, and review after twelve months whether both earned their place. Remember that Premium renews at $299 after year one.

Common traps

  • Treating a Strong Buy rating as a guarantee rather than a summary of data.
  • Reading only the articles that agree with you.
  • Overlooking that fees are generally non-refundable after the trial.
  • Choosing a plan for the investor you wish you were, not the one you are.
  • Skipping the checkout check that confirms $269 before paying.

Checklist

  • Is my portfolio big enough that the fee is a small percentage?
  • Do I understand what is and is not included?
  • Have I checked the checkout total before entering card details?
  • Do I know how to cancel and when renewal happens?
  • Have I ignored any unverified holiday discount claims?
