Why you keep seeing 'student discount' claims

Discount aggregators publish boilerplate Q&A blocks that reuse the same wording across thousands of merchants. One such block we read mentioned redeeming a code 'in retail stores', which Seeking Alpha simply does not have. That tells you the text is generic filler, not a confirmed programme. Seeking Alpha's own help article on long-term discounts only says it runs promotions from time to time and points to its subscriptions page, which in October 2026 shows Premium at $299 per year with no academic option.

Option 1: stay on the free Basic tier

Basic costs nothing and, according to the App Store description, gives you a single Premium article each month, live quotes, a portfolio you build yourself, alerts on tickers you follow and Wall Street consensus ratings. For a learner managing a small account this already covers a lot. Our free vs Premium breakdown shows exactly where the line sits.

Option 2: use the free week wisely

Seeking Alpha gives one free trial per service for life, so burning it during finals is a waste. Start it when you have real hours to research, perhaps during a reporting season or while writing a stock pitch for class. Per the terms, you need to cancel at least a full day before the trial ends if you do not want to be billed.

Option 3: borrow a referral link

If a parent, lecturer or classmate already pays for Premium, their personal referral link gives you fourteen days free instead of seven, and they earn a $25 credit if you later subscribe. See the referral program guide for where that link lives.

Option 4: pay $269 rather than $299

Decide to keep it and the partner route through the Premium offer link trims year one to $269, roughly $22.42 a month. Year two bills at whatever the list price is then, currently $299. No code box is involved.

Option 5: put it on a wish list

Existing account holders can buy someone a year of Premium from the Gifts area of Account Settings, and the person receiving it does not have to hand over card details. Graduation and birthdays are natural moments. The mechanics are in our gift subscription walkthrough.

Does a student actually need Premium?

Be realistic. If your brokerage balance is a few hundred dollars, a $269 annual fee is a huge share of it, and free data plus your library may serve you better. If you are a finance major preparing for interviews, competing in a stock pitch contest or already investing a meaningful sum, features like Quant Ratings, factor and dividend grades, call transcripts and a decade of financial statements can save many hours. Plenty of students trial it for one project and decide afterwards.

Check your campus library first

Some universities license professional finance databases for their students. We cannot say which, if any, include Seeking Alpha, so ask a librarian before you pay for anything.
