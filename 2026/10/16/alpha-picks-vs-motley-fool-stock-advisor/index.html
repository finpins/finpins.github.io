Alpha Picks vs Motley Fool Stock Advisor: two stock-pick services, two picks a month each, very different methods and prices. Here is how to choose. Both services send two new stock recommendations a month and both are built for long-term investors who want ideas handed to them. How Alpha Picks Works

Alpha Picks starts from Seeking Alpha's quant system, which rates stocks on more than 100 metrics against sector peers. From the stocks rated Strong Buy, the service selects two picks each month, published around the 1st and 15th. Subscribers get the full portfolio, Hold and Sell alerts as positions change, and members-only webinars.

Seeking Alpha reports that Alpha Picks returned +356.93% versus +106.24% for the S&P 500 from July 1, 2022 to October 9, 2026. It describes the calculation as a notional portfolio, with entry and exit pricing rules that changed in mid-2024 and a benchmark built from equal SPY purchases on the pick dates with dividends reinvested. This is Seeking Alpha's own claim, not an independent audit. Read more in our Alpha Picks review.

How Motley Fool Stock Advisor Works

Stock Advisor, launched in February 2002, has the Motley Fool's analysts recommend two stocks a month, with rankings and guidance on building a diversified portfolio (the company suggests 25 or more stocks held for the long term). The Motley Fool publishes a long-run return figure for its recommendations versus the S&P 500 on its sales page; like Seeking Alpha's figure, it is a vendor claim. See the Stock Advisor page for current numbers.

Price Compared

Stock Advisor is far cheaper at list price: $199 a year versus $499 for Alpha Picks. The Motley Fool also offers introductory pricing for new members. Seeking Alpha sells Alpha Picks alone or in a bundle with Premium at $718 for the first year, an $80 saving against $798 bought separately.

The Motley Fool offers a 30-day membership-fee-back guarantee. Seeking Alpha's policy is that fees are generally non-refundable unless stated otherwise, though you can cancel at any time and keep access until the end of the paid period.

Method: Quant System vs Human Analysts

The real difference in Alpha Picks vs Motley Fool Stock Advisor is the engine. Alpha Picks is systematic: picks must clear a quantitative Strong Buy bar first, which emphasizes factors like valuation, growth, profitability, momentum and estimate revisions relative to peers. That tends to favor companies whose fundamentals and expectations are improving now.

Stock Advisor is discretionary: analysts look for companies they believe can compound for many years, which can include businesses that do not screen well today. Some investors trust a disciplined model more; others trust experienced human judgment more. Neither guarantees results.

Track Record Length

Stock Advisor has a track record of more than two decades. Alpha Picks has a few years. A longer history covers more market environments, while a shorter one is more influenced by recent conditions. Keep that in mind when comparing headline return claims, and remember both are vendor-calculated.

Portfolio Style

Both encourage long-term holding. Alpha Picks provides explicit Hold and Sell alerts, which helps if you want clear exit guidance. Stock Advisor focuses more on building and holding a broad portfolio. If you hate wondering when to sell, Alpha Picks' alerts may be a meaningful plus.

Research Behind the Picks

If you also subscribe to Seeking Alpha Premium, you can dig into each Alpha Pick with quant ratings, factor grades, contributor analysis and transcripts. That combination is what the bundle is designed for. Stock Advisor includes written rationale for its picks inside the service.
