Budget-based recommendations

Zero budget: use the free tier of Motley Fool Stock Advisor where one exists and Seeking Alpha's Basic account, which includes portfolio tracking, real-time alerts and one premium article a month.

One paid tool: pay for the service that matches your main job. If that job is deciding which individual companies to own, Premium at $269 for year one is the natural pick; if it is the specialty Motley Fool Stock Advisor is known for, choose that.

Two tools: combine a free tier of one with a paid plan of the other, and review after twelve months whether both earned their place. Remember that Premium renews at $299 after year one.

Where people go wrong

  • Signing up by typing the URL, which can bypass link pricing.
  • Expecting PRO-level features from Premium, or paying $2,400 for PRO before testing Premium.
  • Measuring a picks fee in dollars rather than as a share of your portfolio.
  • Not adding holdings on day one, which makes the trial feel empty.
  • Subscribing to two overlapping tools when one free tool would do.

Five questions before you pay

  • Which job am I hiring this for: research, screening, news or picks?
  • Is the free tier of Motley Fool Stock Advisor or Seeking Alpha already enough?
  • Can I test with a trial or guarantee before committing?
  • What will year two cost?
  • Would I notice if I cancelled?
