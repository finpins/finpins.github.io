Alpha Picks vs Motley Fool Stock Advisor (2026 Guide): Which Picks Service?

By FinPins · October 16, 2026

Both services hand you stock picks, but they differ on price and method. Motley Fool Stock Advisor lists at $199 a year; Alpha Picks lists at $499 and is shown at $449 via our offer link (Oct 2026). Alpha Picks draws on Seeking Alpha's quant ratings; Stock Advisor relies on an analyst team. Here is how to choose.

How each picks stocks

Alpha Picks selects from stocks rated Strong Buy by Seeking Alpha's quant system, releasing new picks around the 1st and 15th. Stock Advisor, which The Motley Fool says has run since February 2002, publishes two picks a month from its analyst team.

Cost

Stock Advisor is far cheaper at list: $199 against $499. Alpha Picks can also be bundled with Premium at $718 for year one, an $80 saving versus $798 bought separately, through the Premium + Alpha Picks bundle link.

Track record

Seeking Alpha reports Alpha Picks returned +356.93% versus +106.24% for the S&P 500 from July 1, 2022 to October 9, 2026. Both companies publish their own figures; past performance does not guarantee future results.

Which to choose

Choose Stock Advisor for the lower price and a long history. Choose Alpha Picks if you like a quant-driven method and want to pair picks with Seeking Alpha research. See also Seeking Alpha vs Motley Fool.

Who should skip it

Skip both if you are committed to index funds or your portfolio is small enough that even $199 is a heavy fee.
