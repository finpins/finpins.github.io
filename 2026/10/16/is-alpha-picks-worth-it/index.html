The Performance Claim, Explained

Seeking Alpha reports that Alpha Picks returned +356.93% against +106.24% for the S&P 500 between July 1, 2022 and October 9, 2026. That headline is the main reason people ask whether Alpha Picks is worth it, so it is worth understanding.

According to Seeking Alpha, returns are calculated on a notional portfolio. Until July 30, 2024 picks were entered at the open and exited at the close; from July 31, 2024 the volume-weighted average price is used. The benchmark assumes equal purchases of SPY on each pick date with dividends reinvested. This is a vendor-calculated figure, not an independent audit, and your results will differ depending on when you join, how much you put in each pick and whether you follow every alert. Past performance does not guarantee future results.

The Cost in Context

At $499 a year, Alpha Picks is a meaningful expense. A simple way to think about it: the fee as a percentage of the money you plan to invest in the picks. On a small portfolio, a $499 fee is a large drag that the picks must overcome before you are ahead. On a larger portfolio, it is a smaller percentage. Be honest about how much you will actually allocate.

If you also want Seeking Alpha Premium for research, the bundle at $718 for the first year saves $80 against buying both. See our bundle discount guide and Alpha Picks discount guide.

Who Alpha Picks Is Worth It For

  • Investors who want structure. Two picks a month and clear Hold/Sell alerts remove a lot of decision fatigue.
  • People who trust systematic methods. Picks must first clear Seeking Alpha's quant Strong Buy bar.
  • Premium users who want curation. Premium shows ratings on thousands of stocks; Alpha Picks narrows them to a manageable portfolio.
  • Investors with enough capital that the fee is a small percentage of the amount invested.

Who Should Skip It

  • Index-fund investors who do not want individual stocks.
  • Small portfolios where $499 is a large share of the money invested.
  • Hands-on researchers who prefer building their own list with Premium's tools.
  • Anyone needing a refund window, since Seeking Alpha fees are generally non-refundable.

Alpha Picks vs Just Using Premium

Seeking Alpha Premium costs $299 a year list, or $269 for the first year through our link, and includes Quant Ratings on thousands of stocks, so you can see Strong Buy rated names yourself. What you do not get is the curation: the two monthly selections, portfolio management and Hold/Sell alerts. If you are disciplined and enjoy research, Premium may be enough. If you want a done-for-you portfolio, Alpha Picks adds that layer. Compare in detail in Seeking Alpha Premium vs Alpha Picks.

Risks to Keep in Mind

A concentrated list of stocks can be more volatile than the overall market, and strong past results can attract subscribers right before weaker periods. Quant models can also lag sudden changes in a company's fortunes. Diversify, size positions sensibly and treat Alpha Picks as one input, not your entire plan.

How to Decide

Ask three questions. Will you follow the picks and alerts consistently? Is the fee a small fraction of what you will invest? Do you value a systematic process over picking stocks yourself? If you answer yes to all three, Alpha Picks is likely worth it for you. If not, start with Premium and revisit later.

A Note on Position Sizing

Many subscribers put an equal amount into each new pick, which mirrors how Seeking Alpha's benchmark is built. Whatever you choose, decide your sizing rule in advance so emotions do not drive it.

Is Alpha Picks Worth It? Our Verdict

Is Alpha Picks worth it? For investors who want a structured, quant-driven portfolio with clear sell guidance, and who will invest enough that the $499 fee is modest, it can be good value. For small portfolios, index investors or hands-on researchers, Premium alone is usually the smarter buy. If you want both research and picks, the $718 first-year bundle is the most cost-effective route.
