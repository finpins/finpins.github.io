What TradingView Does Best

TradingView is built around charts. You get fast, customizable charts across stocks, ETFs, forex, crypto and futures, hundreds of built-in indicators, drawing tools, multi-chart layouts and alerts. Its scripting language lets users build and share custom indicators, and its community publishes chart ideas you can follow. Paid tiers raise limits on things like indicators per chart, saved layouts and alerts. On annual billing, the pricing page shows Essential at $12.95 a month, Plus at $29.95 and Premium at $59.95, labeled as special prices that can change; see TradingView pricing.

What Seeking Alpha Does Best

Seeking Alpha is built around companies. Premium unlocks unlimited analysis from its contributor network and earnings call transcripts with audio. The Quant Rating compares each stock with sector peers on more than 100 metrics, scoring it from 1.0 to 5.0, and factor grades for Value, Growth, Profitability, Momentum and EPS Revisions explain the score. Dividend scores, peer comparisons, forecast visuals and ten years of downloadable financials complete the picture.

Fundamental vs Technical: The Real Choice

The Seeking Alpha vs TradingView decision mirrors the oldest split in investing. Fundamental investors ask what a business is worth and whether it is improving. Technical traders ask what price is doing and where it may go next.

If you buy companies to hold for years, a chart tells you little about whether earnings will grow or a dividend is safe. Seeking Alpha answers those questions. If you trade over days or weeks, fundamentals matter less than trend, momentum and levels, and TradingView is the better workbench.

Momentum Inside Seeking Alpha

Seeking Alpha does include a Momentum factor grade in its quant model, which considers price performance relative to sector peers. That is not a charting toolkit, but it gives fundamental investors a sense of whether the market agrees with their thesis. Learn how it fits in our Quant Ratings explainer.

Using Both Together

A popular hybrid approach is to choose what to buy on Seeking Alpha and decide when to buy on TradingView. You shortlist stocks with strong quant ratings and convincing analysis, then use charts to avoid buying right after a sharp spike or to spot support levels. TradingView's free tier is often enough for this kind of light charting, which makes the combination affordable.

A Simple Test

Take one stock you are considering. On Seeking Alpha, note its Quant Rating, its weakest factor grade and the main bear argument from recent analysis. On TradingView, look at the trend over the last year and where key support and resistance sit. Ask which of those notes would actually stop you from buying. That tells you which tool carries more weight in your process.
